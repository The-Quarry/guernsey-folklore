Guernsey Folklore Map of fairies, witches and black dogs

The places where the island's old stories are still attached to the ground: the dolmens where pouques danced, the headland where witches kept their Friday Sabbat, and the lanes walked by headless dogs.

      Know a story?

      If your family tells a tale about a place in Guernsey, Alderney, Sark or Herm, we'd like to hear it. Email us with:

      • the place, as precisely as you can
      • the story, and who told it to you
      • a photo, if you have one, and who took it
      • your name as you'd like it shown, or "anonymous"
      • a line saying we can publish it

      editor@thequarry.media

      We read every story before anything is added to the map. We use your contact details only to reply to you about your story and do not pass them on.

      Stories retold from Sir Edgar MacCulloch's Guernsey Folk Lore (1903), John Linwood Pitts's witch-trial transcripts (1886), Guernsey Museums' folklore stories adapted from Marie de Garis's Folklore of Guernsey, and the sources listed with each entry. Photographs dated 1903 are plates from MacCulloch.

      Many sites are on private land. Please keep to public roads and paths.
