Guernsey Folklore Map of fairies, witches and black dogs

The places where the island's old stories are still attached to the ground: the dolmens where pouques danced, the headland where witches kept their Friday Sabbat, and the lanes walked by headless dogs.

      Help keep these stories alive

      This map is a shared project and it is never finished. You can help by:

      • walking a route and telling us what you find: closed paths, private land, a better way round
      • checking the pins: if a place is in the wrong spot, tell us where it should be
      • sending the stories your family tells, and who told them to you
      • sending photos of the places, old or new, and saying who took them

      Every site and walk has its own "Tell us" link, or email:

      editor@thequarry.media

      We read everything before it is added to the map, and credit you unless you'd rather stay anonymous. We use your contact details only to reply to you about what you sent and do not pass them on.

      Stories retold from Sir Edgar MacCulloch's Guernsey Folk Lore (1903), John Linwood Pitts's witch-trial transcripts (1886), Guernsey Museums' folklore stories adapted from Marie de Garis's Folklore of Guernsey, and the sources listed with each entry. Photographs dated 1903 are plates from MacCulloch.

      Many sites are on private land. Please keep to public roads and paths.
